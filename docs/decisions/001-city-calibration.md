# 001 — One city-level calibration for both corridors

**Date.** 2026-10-24
**Supersedes.** The per-corpus demand models in `research/zone-a/MODEL.md` and
`research/zone-b/MODEL.md`, which remain as the working papers.
**Change.** Every case count, pet base, revenue figure and verdict in the
dossier is computed on one calibration rather than two.

The calibration: the Greater Chennai Corporation's own working figures from the
August to October 2026 vaccination campaign — 1,50,303 dogs vaccinated against
a 2,00,000 target, with roughly one lakh dogs microchipped as a lower bound
— spread over the 1,733,423 households the 2011 Census and the Corporation's
residential property-tax growth give, with veterinary demand at 4.0 contacts per
pet-owning household a year and a built-form animal table calibrated so the two
corridors extrapolate to about 192,000 dogs citywide.

**Why.** The two passes measured demand in different units and against
different household bases. Compared directly they said the OMR corridor was six
times larger than the south-east cluster, which was an artefact. With one
calibration, Zone A holds 11,576 cases a month and Zone B 5,007, and the
ranking is a fact about the corridors rather than about the models.

The tier marks changed with it. Zone A's household base is modelled from a
built-footprint density and is tier C; Zone B's comes from the Census and the
property-tax ledger and is tier B. No figure in the corpus is tier A. The
report says so in the method section rather than implying more confidence than
the inputs carry.
