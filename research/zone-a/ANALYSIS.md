**The one-line reading.** Zone A is a large, dense, well-supplied market that is **not a set of
fifty addressable markets**. Only Velachery main can carry a route on its own. Everywhere else,
the demand is there but it does not fill a 156-visit route at a price that clears Rs 1,100 — and
the eight *route clusters* all do. The commercial decision this dossier supports is a route on a
cluster, not a route on a locality.

**The two southern clusters carry 46.2 percent of the zone's cases and 49.0 percent of its
revenue on 38.8 percent of its households.** A-R7 and A-R5 are the zone. A-R2 — Mangadu, Parandur,

**Four of the eight clusters contain no GO and no HOLD at all.** A-R1 (Tambaram), A-R2 (Mangadu),

and A-R8, the four clusters on the Velachery–Medavakkam–Pallikaranai–Thoraipakkam belt. That is a
clean spatial statement about where the money is, and it is a statement about cluster economics
rather than about twenty-five separate neighbourhoods.

The gradient is mechanical: household count by archetype. A-R7 and A-R5 are where the
`dense_apartment` and `mid_apartment` footprints are; A-R2 is `township`, `industrial` and
out-of-GCC panchayat land.

## 5. The five HOLDs, and the single test that binds

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Medavakkam Town | 305 | Rs 1,099 | Rs 249 | price and margin, by Rs 1 |
| 3 | Pallikaranai (oil mill) | 257 | Rs 1,024 | Rs 174 | price and margin |
| 4 | Velachery Ram Nagar North | 235 | Rs 1,012 | Rs 162 | price and margin |
| 5 | Velachery Jaganathapuram | 216 | Rs 984 | Rs 134 | price and margin |
| 6 | Thoraipakkam | 206 | Rs 956 | Rs 106 | price and margin |

**The single test that binds is the blended price, and only the blended price.** Every HOLD clears
the 40-case gate (206 to 305 cases), clears the saturation gate (23 to 47 against a limit of 70)
and clears the Rs 100 minimum margin (Rs 106 to Rs 249). All five fail because their own demand
will not fill a 156-visit route at a price above Rs 1,100.

That is a single lever with a single fix: **each of the five needs about 20 to 45 fewer
low-value filler visits per route, or a higher-value mix.** In practice it means a plan-led
chronic caseload rather than one-off consultations, and it means the route visits these
localities on a day when the cluster's other stops are also on the round.

**Thoraipakkam is the marginal case.** Its margin is Rs 106, six rupees above the NO-GO minimum.
It is the one HOLD whose verdict is fragile in the other direction: a 6 percent fall in its
household base and it becomes a NO-GO, and a 15 percent rise in its realised price and it becomes
a GO.

## 6. Why 49 localities are below the floor, and why that is the finding

Every one of the fifty localities sits below the Rs 1,100 floor except Velachery main, and 44 sit
below the Rs 100 minimum margin. That is not a modelling artefact and it is not a verdict on the
neighbourhoods. It is what the price floor means when it is applied honestly:

**A route is 156 visits a month.** If a locality's own demand is 60 high-value visits a month, the
other 96 must be filled at Rs 699 routine consultations to make the route worth running, and the
realised price falls to roughly Rs 934. Alandur is exactly that: 187 cases a month and Rs 79,164
of prize, but its own demand pool fills only part of a route, so its blended price is Rs 934 and
its margin is Rs 84.

**At cluster level, every one of the eight rounds clears the floor comfortably** — Rs 1,295 to
Rs 1,311 a visit — because a cluster's combined demand does fill the route. The A-R3 round, which
contains Alandur, realises Rs 1,279 a visit.

So the correct reading of this zone is:

> **The floor is a test on routes, and it binds at the cluster, not at the locality. Zone A
> supports eight route clusters, and it does not support fifty routes.**

Two practical consequences. First, the deployment unit is the cluster round, and the round is
already modelled — eight rounds, eight anchors, Rs 1,295 to Rs 1,311 a visit, all above the
floor. Second, the locality verdict's real job in this zone is to say *which stops go on which
round*, not to say *which round to run*.

**What would change the distribution.** The blend is a function of three things: the locality's own
demand, the margin priority order in which the route is filled, and the Rs 699 filler price. If
the filler visit were priced at Rs 799 rather than Rs 699, Alandur's blend moves from Rs 934 to
about Rs 995 — still short, because a richer filler dilutes less. If the route cap were **120
visits instead of 156**, Alandur's filler share falls and its blend moves to about Rs 1,005, and
Pallikaranai oil mill (Rs 1,024), Velachery Ram Nagar North (Rs 1,012) and Medavakkam Town (Rs
1,099) all cross the floor and become GO — the zone would read 4 GO, 1 HOLD, 45 NO-GO instead of
1, 5, 44. **The verdict distribution is highly sensitive to the route-fill parameters, and that
is the first thing to test.**

Demand itself moves the marginal rows. A 30 percent swing in the household base is worth roughly
Rs 120 of blended price on a mid-sized locality: Medavakkam Town goes from Rs 1,099 to about Rs
1,220 on +30 percent and becomes a GO; Velachery main goes from Rs 1,302 to about Rs 1,250 on
−30 percent and still clears. So the household band flips the HOLDs without rescuing the deep
NO-GOs.

## 8. Hour and weekday shape

The peak window is 09:00–12:00 for the apartment archetypes and 10:00–13:00 for the mixed and
residential ones; the commercial pockets peak at 17:00–20:00. Staffing in the zone summary
concentrates the day into 10:00 to 16:00, with 14 visits an hour at the 10:00 hour falling to 9
at 15:00 and 16:00.

The weekday split is a deliberate shape, not an observation:

```
Mon 0.120  Tue 0.122  Wed 0.124  Thu 0.124  Fri 0.134  Sat 0.184  Sun 0.192
```

That is a **56 percent weekend uplift** on the apartment archetypes, falling to 13.4 percent in the
commercial pockets. The reason is the same on both sides of the model: the GCC licence-renewal
campaign runs through the weekend, the association round lands at the weekend, and the spine
report treats the weekend as an acquisition window and the weekday as a revenue window. If the
practice is to sell annual chronic-care plans, the weekend is when the plan is sold and the
weekday is when it is delivered — and those are two different route shapes, not one route with a
weekend bump.

## 9. Seasonality

The index is the Chennai pattern — a January–February peak for post-campaign renewal demand in
the best weather, a June heat trough, an August monsoon trough, then recovery through the
October–December campaign window — with the apartment archetypes shifted 6 percent up for
March–June and 5 percent up for July–September, because a covered-lobby cluster tolerates a
summer visit that an open-layout locality does not.

| | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun | Jul | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov | Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Velachery main (dense apartment) | 115.5 | 110.6 | 109.0 | 102.9 | 95.7 | 93.6 | 88.7 | 86.6 | 91.7 | 94.1 | 102.9 | 108.7 |
| Kadaperi (industrial) | 125.6 | 120.3 | 102.9 | 97.1 | 90.3 | 88.3 | 80.8 | 78.9 | 83.6 | 102.3 | 111.8 | 118.2 |

The spread between archetypes is the point: the industrial pockets peak harder in January and
trough deeper in August (a swing of 46.7 points) than the apartment clusters (28.9 points), because
an open site in May is not a covered lobby in May. Peak cases a day on the largest locality is 27.

**A repair-pass correction.** This index was published flat and partly negative until this pass:
the scaling had been run through the share normaliser that belongs to the hour curve, which
collapsed every month to the same value and left **January at −120** against a basis text that
says January is 119. The base pattern and the archetype shift were not changed; only the scaling
was corrected, and the index now averages 100.0 in every row.

## 10. The competitive picture

- **Saturation runs 12 to 59** across the fifty, on an index built from operator *type*, not head
  count: a house call scores 12, a clinic with a home-visit arm 9, a public or NGO 7, a platform
  4. The mean competitor count is 4.76.
- **The lightest pockets are also the emptiest.** The twelve localities in Mangadu, Tambaram's
  edge and Hasthinapuram carry a saturation of 12 to 16 — almost no named supply — and they are
  also the localities with the lowest prices and the worst margins. Thin competition in this zone
  is a symptom of thin *premium* demand, not an opening.
- **The GCC free centre is the sharpest competitive fact.** The Meenambakkam pet clinic and ABC
  centre is attached to nine localities (A25–A29, A31, A32, A46, A47) and offers a free
  microchip-and-licence route inside the very cluster the licence-compliance wedge depends on.
  **The wedge the spine report treats as the acquisition engine is available in only 25 of the 50
  addresses in this dataset**, and the free centre sits inside the best part of it.
- **Little Angels' home-visit arm reaches 22 of the 50 localities** and prices a home visit at
  Rs 299. That is the price the our-mix ladder has to beat, and it is why the route fill cannot
  lean on routine visits.
- **The competitive picture is a floor.** 20 of the 50 have no operator named from their own page,
  and 13 of those carry only the three citywide names; Justdial soft-blocked after three requests
  per address and Gully Tails indexes 284 Chennai clinics of which one page loaded.

## 11. What would change the verdict

1. **The route-fill parameters — the biggest lever.** The 49 below-floor localities and the 5
   HOLDs are all set by how a 156-visit route is filled from a locality's own pool and what the
   filler visit costs. A 20 percent change in the route cap, or a Rs 100 change in the filler
   price, moves several rows across a gate. Test this before anything else.
2. **A better household base.** The household figures are tier C — a footprint times an archetype
   density, with no ward-level 2011 household table available for the current 200 divisions. A
   ±30 percent swing moves the blended price of a mid-sized locality by roughly Rs 120, which is
   enough to flip the marginal HOLDs (Medavakkam Town becomes a GO on +30 percent) and not enough
   to bring the deep NO-GOs back. **This is a risk to the ranking and the revenue numbers, and to
   the marginal HOLDs — not to the verdict rule.**
3. **A real price for the plan mix.** The our-mix ladder assumes chronic monitoring under an
   annual plan at Rs 825 against pay-as-you-go at Rs 1,199, and the 55 percent plan share is a
   policy choice. If the real plan mix is 30 percent, the blended price falls and the HOLD set
   grows.
4. **The cluster assignment.** A-R2 is not a cluster. Splitting it and re-testing Parandur and
   Valathur as scattered stops changes their row's economics, not their verdicts.
5. **A live check on the affluence axis.** MagicBricks and propspedia disagree by a factor of
   three on Pammal, and no rent page loaded. If the sale-price median overstates what households
   will actually pay, the market price and the saturation both move.

## 12. The three things to decide next

1. **Design the eight rounds as rounds, and decide which two to open first.** A-R7 (Velachery,
   Rs 63,45,910 a month) and A-R5 (Medavakkam, Rs 44,19,840) carry 49 percent of the zone's
   revenue and both clear the floor at cluster level. A-R4 and A-R6 are the second tier. A-R2
   should not be rostered as one round.
2. **Decide the route cap, because it sets the verdicts.** 156 visits a month is the spine
   report's planning number for a six-day route, and at 156 the filler dilution puts 49 of the 50
   localities under the floor. A 120-visit round or a 130-visit round moves a materially different
   set of localities into GO, and this is a management decision, not a modelling one.
3. **Decide whether the licence-compliance wedge is the entry product in the outer half of the
   zone.** It is not available in 25 of the 50 localities, and the free GCC centre sits inside the
   nine best-connected ones. If the answer is yes for the outer half, the acquisition product has
   to be something else — the chronic-care plan and the senior wellness bundle — and the route
   economics in §5 have to be built on those lines instead.
